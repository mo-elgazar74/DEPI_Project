# Keep overlap of 50–100 tokens

## Summary

Maintain a token overlap of 50–100 between consecutive text chunks during the [[RagSystemRoadmap/ChunkingMethods.md|chunking]] process to preserve contextual meaning and prevent information loss at chunk boundaries, which is crucial for high-quality [[RagSystemRoadmap/SemanticSearch.md|semantic search]] results.

## Key concepts

*   **Chunk Overlap**: The number of tokens that are repeated at the end of one text chunk and the beginning of the next. Think of it like overlapping shingles on a roof; each shingle covers part of the one below it to ensure no gaps for water (or information) to leak through.
    *   Example: If a document is split into Chunk A (tokens 1-500) and Chunk B (tokens 451-950), the overlap is 50 tokens (tokens 451-500).
*   **Token**: A basic unit of text, which can be a word, subword, or punctuation mark, that an [[RagSystemRoadmap/EmbeddingGeneration.md|embedding model]] processes. For instance, the sentence "The cat sat." might be tokenized into ["The", "cat", "sat", "."].
    *   Example: Using a tokenizer to see the breakdown: `tokenizer.encode("RAG systems are useful.")` might output `[1923, 342, 587, 12]`, representing 4 tokens.

## Why it matters

*   **Preserves Context**: Prevents the "context boundary problem," where a key piece of information is split across two chunks, making it invisible to the [[RagSystemRoadmap/SemanticSearch.md|retrieval system]] when searching from a single chunk. A 50–100 token overlap acts as a safety buffer.
    *   Analogy: It's like reading a book and re-reading the last paragraph of the previous page when you turn the page; it helps maintain the flow of ideas and prevents you from missing crucial connections.
*   **Improves Retrieval Accuracy**: By ensuring that concepts which span a natural break in the text are fully contained within at least one chunk, you increase the likelihood of retrieving the most relevant information for a user's query.
    *   Example: A question about "the causes of the economic event described at the end of the chapter" might require context from the end of one chunk and the beginning of the next. Overlap ensures this context is captured wholly in one chunk.

## Core steps

*   **Configure Your Chunker**: When setting up your text splitter (like the [[RagSystemRoadmap/ChunkingRegexRecursiveSplitter.md|RecursiveSplitter]]), explicitly set the `chunk_overlap` parameter to a value between 50 and 100. This action instructs the splitting algorithm to deliberately duplicate tokens between adjacent chunks.
    *   Example: In Python, with a common text splitting library:
        ````python
        from langchain.text_splitter import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter
        
        text_splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
            chunk_size = 500,
            chunk_overlap = 50  # Action: Set 50-token overlap
        )
        ````
*   **Validate Overlap in Sample Chunks**: After processing a document, inspect the first few chunks to confirm the overlap is present and within the target range. This verification step catches configuration errors early in the [[RagSystemRoadmap/Phase2PreprocessingChunking.md|preprocessing phase]].
    *   Example: Print the start/end of consecutive chunks and manually check the overlapping text:
        ````python
        chunks = text_splitter.split_text(long_document)
        print("Chunk 1 end:", chunks[0][-100:]) # Last ~100 chars of chunk 1
        print("Chunk 2 start:", chunks[1][:100]) # First ~100 chars of chunk 2
        # These two strings should have a significant common substring.
        ````
